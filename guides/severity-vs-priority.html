Severity vs priority: decide with impact and timing, not labels

Severity asks how badly the defect affects the product or user. Priority asks how urgently the team should address it. They influence each other, but they are not the same decision.

A practical decision frame

Severity inputs: scope of impact, data loss, blocked workflow, security/privacy exposure, accessibility barrier and availability of a workaround.

Priority inputs: release timing, number of affected users, business criticality, legal/compliance deadline, support load, workaround cost and fix risk.

Four scenarios

High severity · High priority

Checkout charges the customer but order creation fails. Core revenue flow and reconciliation are at risk.

High severity · Lower immediate priority

A destructive admin-only workflow fails in a feature disabled for all production tenants. Impact is severe if enabled, urgency may be lower today.

Low severity · High priority

A typo appears in the company name on a homepage launching in a national campaign tomorrow. Functionality is intact; business urgency is high.

Low severity · Low priority

A minor alignment issue appears in a rarely used secondary panel with no usability impact.

Triage worksheet

  • What user goal is affected?
  • How many users or roles can hit it?
  • Is there a safe workaround?
  • What happens if we ship it?
  • What deadline makes timing important?
  • What evidence supports the chosen rating?

Pick three findings in the QA Lab. Give each a severity and a separate priority, then write one sentence defending each decision.
